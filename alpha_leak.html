Info — how the strategies work

Arbbet — Spot + Put + NO

Buy 1 ETH spot, buy N Dec-2026 puts (strike below spot) on Bybit, and buy NO shares on Polymarket on the "ETH reach $X by 31 Dec 2026" market (X above spot). The put pays out if ETH falls; the NO pays out if ETH doesn't reach X. Pick N so the worst case is still positive — that's the "arbitrage" window.

Fee model (net of fees)

Every P&L is after fees: spot buy + sell = 2× your spot fee, put open + close = 2× your options fee (Bybit options: 0.03% of underlying, capped at 12.5% of premium), NO buy + sell = 2× your Polymarket fee (0 if you hold to resolution). Set a rate to 0 to ignore it.

GC ↔ GLD converter

Polymarket NO is priced on GC (CME gold, $/oz). GLD is a SPDR ETF — 1 share tracks ~1/10 oz of gold. The converter gives you the oz-per-GLD ratio so you can compare a GLD-based NO against the GC strike. GLD price is auto from Stooq — edit to override.

NO bet — sizing

Pick a NO strike (or type manually) — then Auto sizes N so the return is positive at every outcome: the put covers the downside, the NO covers the upside. No strike listed → no feasible combo at that entry.

Safe range (shown under the N input): Min N — crash side, the NO payout must cover the loss down to the put strike: N > (spot + premium − K_put) / (100 − NO price). Max N — rally side, the NO cost must stay below the gain up to the NO strike: N < (max(K_NO, K_put) − spot − premium) / NO price. Balanced N = (K_NO − K_put)/100 maximizes the worst case. If the put strike is above the NO strike, the worst case is K_put − spot − N×NO price, so the smallest N is best.

GLD put data

The GLD Dec-2026 puts are an embedded CBOE snapshot — CBOE has no CORS, so they can't be fetched live. To refresh, paste a fresh Dec-2026 GLD put list (e.g. [[420,20.00,20.65,2282],[425,22.40,23.15,1729],…] = [strike, bid, ask, OI]) into the ①b converter card in the Arbbet tab.

Entry sweep

Premium = live Bybit Dec-2026 put ask at each entry price (interpolated). NO price = live Polymarket ask (same market for every row). Green = always-positive combo exists at that entry; red = no feasible N. The sweep answers: "if I had entered at $2,400 instead of today, what would I have done?"

Cash & Carry — spot long + 1× short future

Buy 1 BTC spot and use it as margin to short 1× BTC in the Coin-M delivery future (contract price > spot = contango). At delivery the future settles to spot: the short leg closes at the same price the spot leg bought at, so the position is delta-neutral and you keep the basis = future − spot as risk-free-ish yield (you still carry liquidation risk if the price gaps past your margin). Result shown as APY to the contract's delivery date. All dollar figures are per $1,000 of capital (capital = spot + initial margin) — return % is the same at any size.

Setup

Buy 1 BTC spot @ $S, post it as collateral, short 1 BTC delivery future @ $F (F > S).

At delivery

The future settles to the final spot index. Your short leg closes at exactly the price your spot leg is worth → net price exposure ≈ 0. You keep F − S (the basis) minus fees — regardless of where BTC ends up, as long as you survive to delivery.

Risk

If the price gaps up more than your free margin can absorb, the short leg gets liquidated and you're left long-only spot. Initial margin 20% / maintenance 2.5% (tier-1) leaves a wide buffer, but a sustained rally against you is the failure mode — that's the trade-off for the "risk-free" carry.

Fee model (net of fees)

Every P&L is after fees: spot buy + sell = 2× your spot fee, futures open (taker) — the short is closed by delivery settlement, no fee. Set a rate to 0 to ignore it. Capital = spot + initial margin (20% of notional). The margin is not extra cost — it's your own spot value locked up — but it IS capital at work, so APY is measured on it.
